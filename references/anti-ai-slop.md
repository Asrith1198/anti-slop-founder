# Anti-AI-Slop Checklist

Run every deliverable against this before presenting it. If something on this list shows up, don't soften it or explain it away — cut it and generate a real alternative.

## Visual clichés to reject on sight

- **The purple-to-blue (or blue-to-pink) gradient background**, especially behind a "futuristic" headline. This is the single most overused visual signature of generic AI/tech branding.
- **A circle containing an abstract swoosh, blob, or orbit line.** If the first logo idea is "circle + abstract shape inside," treat that as a placeholder, not a finished concept, and generate a structurally different second idea.
- **A stylized brain, circuit board, or neural-net pattern** to represent "AI" or "smart." Literal.
- **Neumorphism / soft-UI drop shadows on everything** (the puffy, embossed-looking cards). Dated and generic.
- **Glassmorphism used as decoration rather than function** (frosted-glass panels with no purpose beyond "looking modern").
- **Generic rounded-corner sans-serif on rounded-corner cards with a soft pastel background** — the default "friendly SaaS" look that now reads as templated precisely because it's the default.
- **Stock lightbulb, rocket, or handshake icons** standing in for "ideas," "growth," or "partnership."
- **A literal money/coin/wallet icon for anything fintech-adjacent** — see logo-concepts.md, this category has moved to abstract marks.
- **Chef hats, forks-and-knives crossed, or steam-lines for food brands** — see logo-concepts.md, specificity beats genre iconography.

## Typographic clichés to reject

- **Inter (or Inter + Poppins, or Montserrat + Open Sans) as the unexamined default** regardless of what the brand actually is. These are fine fonts that have become invisible through overuse — using them without a specific reason is the typographic equivalent of the purple gradient.
- **All-lowercase branding used reflexively** to signal "approachable/modern" with no other differentiation backing it up.
- **A script font used for an entire block of body text** (illegible, and it's almost always a food/lifestyle-brand mistake).

## Naming/copy clichés to reject

- **Tech-startup suffix soup:** slapping "-ify," "-ly," "-io," "-hub," "-ly," "AI" onto a generic root word and calling it a name. (Covered in depth in naming.md's SCRATCH filter — "Tame" and "Annoying.")
- **"Revolutionizing," "seamless," "cutting-edge," "next-generation," "empowering," "unlock your potential."** Generic marketing filler that could apply to literally any product in any category — if the copy would still make sense with the brand name swapped for a competitor's, it hasn't said anything specific.
- **A tagline that just restates the category** ("The Future of X," "X, Reimagined") without saying what's actually different.

## Structural/process clichés to reject

- **Presenting exactly three options, all minor variations of the same idea**, and calling it a range of choices. Real range means genuinely different directions (see style-archetypes.md), not three fonts in the same weight.
- **A single confident answer to "what should this look like" with no reasoning shown**, OR the opposite failure — hedging every suggestion so hard ("it could be this, or maybe this, really it depends") that nothing is actually recommended. Both are failures of taste; commit to a point of view, and back it with a reason.
- **Copying a specific competitor's actual visual system** rather than referencing the *category feeling* it belongs to. Reference brands in this skill (Kiro, Nvidia, Souled Store, etc.) are orientation points for a feeling, never templates to trace.

## The test before presenting anything

Ask: **"If I saw this exact output from ten different AI tools today, would it look different each time, or identical?"** If the honest answer is "identical," it's slop — go back to the specific brief (the product, the audience, the actual story) and pull something out of it that couldn't have come from any other brand.
