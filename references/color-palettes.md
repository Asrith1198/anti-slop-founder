# Color Palettes by Archetype

Every palette below has six roles: **Primary** (the brand color), **Secondary** (a supporting color for the primary's system), **Accent** (used sparingly, for CTAs/highlights), **Neutral-Dark**, **Neutral-Light**, **Background**. Always give hex codes, never just color names — "a confident blue" is not usable.

These are starting points to remix, not the only six palettes on Earth. When generating a genuinely new palette, use the "Building a palette from scratch" rules at the bottom.

---

### 1. Corporate Systematic
- Primary `#1A73E8` (confident blue) · Secondary `#0B3D91` (deep navy) · Accent `#FBBC04` (warm signal yellow, used only for alerts/CTAs)
- Neutral-Dark `#1F2430` · Neutral-Light `#F1F3F4` · Background `#FFFFFF`
- Logic: one saturated primary carries brand recognition; the yellow accent exists only to draw the eye to one action per screen — never decorative.

### 2. AI-Native Minimal
- Primary `#141413` (near-black, warm not cool) · Secondary `#D97757` (muted terracotta — think warm clay, not tech-blue) · Accent `#CC785C`
- Neutral-Dark `#2B2A27` · Neutral-Light `#E8E6DC` (warm off-white, not `#FFFFFF`) · Background `#FAF9F5`
- Logic: warm neutrals instead of stark white/black is what separates this from generic "tech" — cool grays read corporate, warm grays read considered.

### 3. Quirky-Technical / Indie Dev
- Primary `#0D0D0D` (true near-black base) · Secondary `#5E6AD2` (electric indigo, used as the single hero accent) · Accent `#00FF9C` (acid mint, tiny doses only — status/success states)
- Neutral-Dark `#1C1C1F` · Neutral-Light `#8A8F98` · Background `#0D0D0D` or `#FFFFFF` (pick one mode as primary, support the other)
- Logic: dark-mode-first; the indigo does the branding work, the mint is a UI-state color, not a brand color — don't let them compete.

### 4. Streetwear / Mascot Rebel
- Primary `#0A0A0A` (ink black) · Secondary `#FFFFFF` · Accent `#D7263D` (blood red) or swap accent for `#FFC700` (hazard yellow) depending on mood
- Neutral-Dark `#0A0A0A` · Neutral-Light `#E5E5E5` · Background `#F5F5F0` (slightly off-white, like raw canvas/paper)
- Logic: two-color-plus-one-punch — resist adding a fourth color. The restriction is what makes it feel like a mark you'd actually wear.

### 5. Stylish Performance-Tech
- Primary `#0B0E11` (void black) · Secondary `#76B900`-style acid green *or* `#C9A24B` muted gold (pick one lane, not both) · Accent same as secondary, used at higher saturation for CTAs
- Neutral-Dark `#15181C` · Neutral-Light `#9CA3AF` · Background `#0B0E11`
- Logic: near-monochrome base with exactly one "warning light" color doing all the emotional work — this is the opposite of the food-brand approach of many warm colors at once.

### 6. Playful Edtech / Student
- Primary `#58CC02` (energetic green) · Secondary `#1CB0F6` (sky blue) · Accent `#FF9600` (warm orange) + `#FF4B4B` (used sparingly for streaks/urgency)
- Neutral-Dark `#3C3C3C` · Neutral-Light `#F7F7F7` · Background `#FFFFFF`
- Logic: unlike most archetypes here, this one *wants* 3-4 active colors — it reads as energy, not inconsistency, as long as each color maps to a consistent meaning (e.g. green = progress, orange = streak/urgency).

### 7. Warm Food & Hospitality
- Primary `#C1440E` (burnt terracotta) · Secondary `#F2A65A` (turmeric/mustard) · Accent `#7A9D54` (herb green, used for fresh/veg cues)
- Neutral-Dark `#3A2618` (coffee brown, used instead of black) · Neutral-Light `#F6E8D8` (cream) · Background `#FBF3E7`
- Logic: warm neutrals throughout, including swapping pure black for a coffee-brown — pure black next to warm colors reads clinical, which fights the "warm/handmade" goal.

### 8. Heritage / Craft
- Primary `#7A2E2E` (oxide red, brick/terracotta pottery tone) · Secondary `#C89B3C` (aged brass/gold) · Accent `#2E4034` (deep forest, used for grounding contrast)
- Neutral-Dark `#2B211B` · Neutral-Light `#E8DCC8` (unbleached cotton) · Background `#F3EAD8`
- Logic: every color should be nameable as a real material (brick, brass, cotton, forest) — if a color can't be tied to something tactile, it doesn't belong in this palette.

### 9. Civic / Hackathon Energy
- Primary `#FF3E30` (alarm/urgency red) *or* `#0057FF` (movement blue) — pick one, this archetype wants a single loud primary · Secondary `#111111` · Accent `#FFFFFF`
- Neutral-Dark `#111111` · Neutral-Light `#EAEAEA` · Background `#FFFFFF`
- Logic: near black-white-and-one-siren-color — needs to read instantly on a stage screen from 30 feet away, so complexity is the enemy here.

### 10. Fintech Trust
- Primary `#0A2540` (deep trust navy) · Secondary `#00D4B0` (modern teal-green, the one "alive" note) · Accent `#00D4B0` used at full saturation for CTAs only
- Neutral-Dark `#1A2B3C` · Neutral-Light `#E3E8EE` · Background `#F6F9FC`
- Logic: navy carries the "safe with your money" signal; exactly one bright accent keeps it from feeling like a bank from 1998.

### 11. Brutalist / Indie Maximalist
- Primary `#000000` · Secondary `#FFFFFF` · Accent `#FF3300` (a deliberately "wrong"/clashing red-orange) — the wrongness is the point, don't soften it toward something tasteful
- Neutral-Dark `#000000` · Neutral-Light `#FFFFFF` · Background `#FFFFFF`
- Logic: true black/white, no in-between grays — grays would soften the confrontation this archetype is going for.

### 12. Luxury Minimal
- Primary `#0B0B0B` · Secondary `#F5F5F0` (bone white) · Accent `#B08D57` (muted brass/gold, used maybe once per page)
- Neutral-Dark `#0B0B0B` · Neutral-Light `#C9C9C4` · Background `#FFFFFF` or `#0B0B0B` (dark and light versions both work; pick one as canonical)
- Logic: the accent should appear so rarely it feels like a reward for looking closely, not a color you'd describe as "part of the palette" at a glance.

## Building a new palette from scratch (when none of the above fits)

1. **One primary, chosen for a reason** — not "I like blue," but "blue because X competitor owns red and we need differentiation" or "terracotta because it's tied to the physical product."
2. **One secondary that either harmonizes (analogous) or deliberately contrasts (complementary)** — pick on purpose, state which.
3. **One accent, used at <10% of visual weight** — its whole job is to draw the eye to one thing. If it's used everywhere, it's not an accent, it's a second primary, and you've made a 2-primary palette by accident.
4. **Neutrals should match the temperature of the primary** — a warm primary (red/orange/terracotta) wants warm-toned grays (grays with a hint of brown), a cool primary wants cool-toned grays. Mismatched temperature is one of the most common "why does this look slightly off" causes in amateur palettes.
5. **Check contrast ratios** for text-on-background pairs before finalizing (aim for WCAG AA — 4.5:1 for body text) — a palette that fails accessibility isn't done yet.
