# Logo Concepts by Archetype

Every entry gives at least one **wordmark** direction and one **mark/symbol** direction (many brands want both — a symbol that works standalone as an app icon/favicon, plus a full lockup with the name). Descriptions should be concrete enough that a designer could sketch from them without more input: what shape, built from what, how it reduces at 32px.

---

### 1. Corporate Systematic
- **Wordmark:** clean geometric sans, tight letter-spacing, often set in a single weight across the whole product family for consistency.
- **Mark:** a simple geometric shape built on a strict grid (circle, square, or a small set of primitive shapes recombined) — designed explicitly to work as a *system* (the same base shape recolors/recombines across sub-products), not a one-off illustration.
- **Reduces to:** a single flat-color glyph, no gradients, reads instantly at favicon size.

### 2. AI-Native Minimal
- **Wordmark:** lowercase or sentence-case, warm sans or sans/serif hybrid, unusually generous letter-spacing — the *restraint* is the design decision.
- **Mark:** an abstract, slightly organic geometric form (an asterisk-like burst, a soft-cornered polygon, a single continuous line) — avoid anything that reads as literally "a brain," "a circuit," or "a neural net," which is the single most overused visual cliché in this category right now.
- **Reduces to:** the mark alone should feel calm even at 16px — if it's busy at small sizes, simplify further.

### 3. Quirky-Technical / Indie Dev
- **Wordmark:** often set in or accented with monospace, precise and small, sometimes lowercase.
- **Mark:** a single, very simple geometric shape rendered with unusual precision (a triangle, a specific angle, a shape derived from the product's actual function) — the wit is in how *perfectly* simple it is, not in adding more elements. Think "one shape, executed with obsessive attention to the curve/angle," the way a well-made dev tool logo often is just a triangle or a specific polygon nobody else uses.
- **Reduces to:** works as a single-color favicon with zero loss of recognizability — this archetype's mark should be simple enough to redraw from memory.

### 4. Streetwear / Mascot Rebel
- **Wordmark:** bold, often condensed or slab, sometimes distressed/hand-touched.
- **Mark:** a **character/mascot** with a specific expression and attitude — not a generic devil/skull, but one with a defined personality (grinning, winking, mid-action). Give it a *pose*, not just a face — a mascot doing something (leaning, pointing, mid-laugh) reads as more crafted than a static front-facing head.
- **Reduces to:** should work as a sticker/patch/embroidery — test the concept by imagining it screen-printed on a black t-shirt at 3 inches.

### 5. Stylish Performance-Tech
- **Wordmark:** condensed, angular, often all-caps, tight tracking.
- **Mark:** an angular geometric symbol built from cut corners, chevrons, or a single diagonal "slash" motif — should feel like it has implied motion/direction even though it's static (think of how a chevron or an angled wing-shape reads as "fast" without moving).
- **Reduces to:** a single bold-colored glyph on near-black — test that it doesn't disappear against dark UI, which is where this archetype usually lives.

### 6. Playful Edtech / Student
- **Wordmark:** rounded sans, often with one letter given a small illustrative twist (a dot replaced with a shape, one letterform slightly customized) rather than a fully custom typeface.
- **Mark:** a rounded mascot or character with big, expressive features — should have a name and a one-line personality (e.g. "an owl that's a little too intense about your streak"), not just be "a cute animal."
- **Reduces to:** the mascot's *face alone* (not full body) should work as an app icon — test at that constraint.

### 7. Warm Food & Hospitality
- **Wordmark:** a script or hand-touched accent for the primary logotype, paired with a clean sans for a secondary/functional lockup (menus, packaging fine print).
- **Mark:** derived from something *specific* to the actual food/product — a real ingredient's shape, a real cooking vessel, a real regional motif — rather than a generic fork/knife/chef-hat icon. Specificity is what separates a memorable food-brand mark from stock iconography.
- **Reduces to:** should still read warm even in single-color (for packaging print constraints, which are common in food) — test in one flat color before finalizing.

### 8. Heritage / Craft
- **Wordmark:** serif or a modernized culturally-rooted script, set inside or alongside a badge/seal shape.
- **Mark:** a **stamp, seal, or badge construction** — a circular or shield frame containing a simplified symbol tied to the brand's actual origin story (a real motif, tool, or pattern from the craft/region, simplified to line work).
- **Reduces to:** the seal shape often *is* the favicon — badges are naturally suited to small circular formats.

### 9. Civic / Hackathon Energy
- **Wordmark:** bold condensed, often all-caps, built for banner/stage legibility.
- **Mark:** a **badge, shield, or flag-derived shape** — but built around the *specific* theme of the event/cause rather than a stock civic seal (e.g. a shape that references the actual issue the hackathon addresses, not a generic government-style crest).
- **Reduces to:** must be legible as a silhouette alone (single flat color, no detail) — test by squinting at it.

### 10. Fintech Trust
- **Wordmark:** clean geometric sans, precise, often with a subtly customized letterform (many fintech logos quietly modify one letter — a cut corner, a connected pair of letters).
- **Mark:** **abstract geometric only** — explicitly avoid literal currency symbols, coins, or wallets, which read as dated/generic in this category now. An abstract shape built from two overlapping or interlocking forms (implying "connection" or "flow" without being literal) works well.
- **Reduces to:** single flat-color glyph, must remain crisp at the tiny sizes used in payment UI (16-24px) — this is one of the most size-constrained use cases of any archetype here.

### 11. Brutalist / Indie Maximalist
- **Wordmark:** IS the logo, often — oversized type, sometimes intentionally cropped/overflowing its container, no separate symbol needed.
- **Mark (if any):** a raw geometric shape (a single black square, an unrefined circle) used more as a graphic device than a "designed" symbol — refinement itself would undercut the point.
- **Reduces to:** should look intentional even when "broken" (cropped, overlapping) — the confidence of the crop is the design.

### 12. Luxury Minimal
- **Wordmark:** thin-weight, wide letter-spacing, often the *entire* identity (many luxury-minimal brands skip a separate symbol entirely and let the wordmark alone be the mark).
- **Mark (if any):** reduced to the single most essential line/shape possible — if you can remove an element and the mark still reads, remove it. The test for this archetype is always "what's the least I can keep."
- **Reduces to:** at favicon scale, often just an initial letterform rather than a full symbol — simplicity all the way down.

---

## General logo construction rules

1. **Every mark must work in one flat color.** If a concept only works with a gradient or multiple colors, it's not finished — test the black-and-white version first, always.
2. **Test at three sizes before presenting:** billboard/hero (huge), app icon (32-64px), favicon (16px). A concept that only works at one size isn't done.
3. **A mascot needs a name and a specific pose/expression**, not just "a friendly character." Vague mascots are the fastest route to generic output.
4. **An abstract mark needs a stated reason for its shape** — "it's derived from the first letter" or "it references [specific thing about the product/culture]" — an abstract shape chosen for no reason reads as filler, even if it looks fine.
5. **Don't default to a circle-with-abstract-swoosh-inside.** This is the single most common AI-slop logo shape right now (see anti-ai-slop.md) — if the first idea is a circle containing an abstract line/blob, generate a second, structurally different concept before presenting anything.
