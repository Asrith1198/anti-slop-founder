# Messaging & Verbal Identity

A brand isn't just a name and a logo — it's also what it *says* and how it *sounds*. This file is the bridge between the other two phases: it's where Phase 3's research (the market-gap thesis, the actual complaints real users had) turns into the words the brand uses, and where Phase 2's archetype gets a voice to match its visuals.

**Always ground positioning in evidence when it exists.** If `idea-validation.md` or `survey-design.md` has already run, pull the market-gap thesis and the sharpest real pain quotes from there — the differentiation claim should be the thing research actually found, not a generic claim that could apply to any competitor. If validation hasn't run yet, ground it in `idea-discovery.md`'s founder-fit angle instead, and flag that the positioning is provisional until the thesis is tested.

## The core deliverables

**1. Value proposition (one sentence)**
Formula: *"[Product] helps [specific audience] [achieve specific outcome] by [specific mechanism] — unlike [status quo/competitor], which [specific failure]."*
The last clause is what most value props skip, and it's the one that actually differentiates. "Helps people manage tasks" is not a value proposition — it's a category description. "Helps students split a messy syllabus into daily tasks automatically by voice, unlike generic to-do apps, which leave the breakdown work to the user" is one.

**2. Elevator pitch (3-4 sentences, said out loud in under 20 seconds)**
Structure: the problem (specific, ideally with a real stat or quote from validation) → the current broken way people solve it → what this does differently → why now (the timing unlock, if one exists from idea-discovery.md). Write it to be *spoken*, not read — short clauses, no subordinate-clause pileups.

**3. Tagline**
3-6 words, sits under the logo. This is the highest-risk-of-cliché deliverable in the whole skill — run every candidate against the naming/copy section of `anti-ai-slop.md` before presenting ("Revolutionizing X," "The Future of X," "X, Reimagined" are all automatic rejects). A good tagline either states the mechanism ("Split by voice, not by hand"), states the emotional payoff, or makes a specific promise — never a vague aspiration. Generate 8-10, don't present the first one.

**4. Messaging pillars (2-4 max)**
The handful of claims the brand needs people to believe, each backed by a reason-to-believe. Format per pillar: *Claim → why it's true → what it rules out.* More than 4 pillars means nothing is actually prioritized — cut, don't add.

**5. The "why us" differentiation statement**
One sentence, explicitly naming what's different from the specific competitors `idea-validation.md` found — not a generic "we care more" claim. If validation hasn't run, this has to stay provisional and say so rather than inventing a differentiation nobody's checked.

## Tone of voice, by archetype

Pulls from the same 12 archetypes as `style-archetypes.md` — pick whichever one is already driving the visual identity, keep voice and visuals consistent.

| Archetype | Voice | Says | Doesn't say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate Systematic | Precise, confident, low-adjective | "Deploys in under a minute." | "Revolutionary new platform!" |
| AI-Native Minimal | Calm, plainspoken, slightly understated | "It just works the way you'd expect." | "Unleash the power of AI." |
| Quirky-Technical | Dry, specific, a little deadpan | "Yes, it does that. No, we don't know why you'd need it either, but here it is." | Forced exclamation points |
| Streetwear/Mascot Rebel | Loud, slangy, in on the joke | "Built different. Wear it loud." | Corporate-safe hedging |
| Stylish Performance-Tech | Terse, declarative, confident | "Faster. Every time." | Long explanatory sentences |
| Playful Edtech/Student | Warm, encouraging, a little cheeky | "You've got this. Let's break it down." | Condescending "simplified for you" framing |
| Warm Food & Hospitality | Sensory, specific, generous | "Made the way your grandmother would've made it — just faster." | Generic "delicious" with no specifics |
| Heritage/Craft | Storytelling, unhurried, rooted | "Three generations of the same recipe, now in your city." | Trend-chasing slang |
| Civic/Hackathon | Urgent, inclusive, rallying | "Built by the people who live the problem." | Bureaucratic/official-sounding copy |
| Fintech Trust | Clear, precise, zero jargon | "Your money, where you can see it, always." | Vague reassurance ("bank-grade security" with no specifics) |
| Brutalist/Indie Maximalist | Blunt, unpolished, confrontational | "We didn't make this nice. We made it work." | Soft, apologetic hedging |
| Luxury Minimal | Spare, understated, confident by omission | "Less. On purpose." | Over-explaining the obvious |

## Quality bar

- Every claim traceable to either real research (Phase 3) or a real, stated founder-fit reason (Phase 1) — never a generic marketing claim invented to sound good.
- Read every pitch/tagline out loud before presenting — if it sounds like it was written to be read, not said, rewrite it.
- Run the full set against `anti-ai-slop.md`'s naming/copy clichés section before presenting — "seamless," "empowering," "cutting-edge," "unlock your potential" are automatic rewrites.
