# Naming — Methodology by Vertical

Core methodology (SMILE/SCRATCH, sound symbolism, generation techniques) applies everywhere — see below. This file leads with **which techniques to weight more heavily per vertical**, since a corporate B2B brief and a streetwear brand should not be generated the same way.

Always generate in volume first (15-25+ candidates), then filter — see "The Naming Funnel" below. Never present a single name as the only option unless explicitly asked to just pick one.

---

## By vertical

### Corporate / B2B
Lean toward: **root-word mining** (Greek/Latin roots read credible — Xerox from "xeros"), **real-word or compound names** (left side of the Naming Matrix — Salesforce, Accenture-style coined-but-serious), abstract names when the company needs room to expand beyond one product category.
Avoid: cutesy wordplay, anything that limits future category expansion (the "Restrictive" SCRATCH flag — e.g. "Boston Market" boxed itself into a city and a format).
Tone target: Distinctive but trustworthy — should sound credible in a pitch deck and on a $10M invoice both.

### Student / Community
Lean toward: **real-word names with strong imagery**, **portmanteaus** (blend two relevant words), names with built-in "legs" for merch/community culture (a name people would want on a hoodie or as a Discord handle).
Avoid: anything institutional-sounding (kills the peer-to-peer trust these brands run on), anything that only makes sense to insiders on day one (Curse of Knowledge flag) unless the insider-ness *is* the point (a campus-specific in-joke can work for a tight community).
Tone target: Should feel like something a student would say out loud to a friend, not something a university administration would approve.

### Food
Lean toward: **sound symbolism for appetite/warmth** (soft sounds — l, m, n — read approachable; avoid harsh plosive-heavy names for comfort food, though plosives can work for bold/spicy positioning), real ingredient or regional references, names that photograph/print well on packaging and menus.
Avoid: names that are hard to say out loud when ordering (a food name people mispronounce is a name people avoid ordering), anything that reads clinical.
Tone target: Should make someone hungry or curious within one read.

### AI / Tech Product
Lean toward: **abstract or suggestive names** (right side of the Naming Matrix has room here — Claude, Anthropic itself lean suggestive-to-abstract), short and easy to say as a verb ("I asked Claude," "let me X it"), names that don't over-promise a specific narrow capability (so the product can grow).
Avoid: literal tech-cliché roots (neuro-, cogni-, -ify, -io, -AI suffix stacking — see anti-ai-slop.md), names that will sound dated once the current AI-naming trend passes (many "-ify"/"-io" names from 2015 already read dated; the current wave of stacked "AI"-suffix names will likely do the same).
Tone target: Should sound like it could still be the name in ten years, not "clearly named during an AI hype cycle."

### Streetwear / Lifestyle
Lean toward: **free association** (idioms, slang, cultural references), names that work as a standalone graphic/wordmark, short and punchy (fits on a small tag/patch), names with attitude baked in.
Avoid: anything that sounds like it's trying to be edgy without committing (forced wordplay — the "Annoying" SCRATCH flag hits this category hardest).
Tone target: Should sound like something that already has a following, even before it does.

### Civic / Hackathon
Lean toward: **compound names combining the cause + action** (clear and mission-forward beats clever here), names that work as a hashtag/URL (hackyatra.in-style — short, no ambiguity, memorable when shouted across a room).
Avoid: overly abstract names (civic/community brands need instant legibility of *what this is*, more than most other categories), long names that get truncated awkwardly on banners.
Tone target: Should be understandable and rally-able within one hearing — this is the one vertical where "descriptive-leaning-suggestive" usually beats pure abstraction.

## Core Methodology (applies to all verticals)

### The SMILE & SCRATCH Framework (Alexandra Watkins)
> "A name should make you smile instead of scratch your head. If it makes you scratch your head, scratch it off the list."

**SMILE — 5 qualities of a great name**
| Letter | Quality | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | Suggestive | Evokes something about the brand | Amazon suggests vastness |
| M | Memorable | Connects to something familiar | Apple connects to something everyone knows |
| I | Imagery | Aids memory through evocative visuals | Jaguar creates an immediate mental picture |
| L | Legs | Extends into wordplay/brand mythology | Nike enables "Just Do It" |
| E | Emotional | Moves people | Patagonia evokes adventure |

**SCRATCH — 7 deal-breakers**
| Letter | Deal-breaker | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| S | Spelling-challenged | Looks like a typo |
| C | Copycat | Sounds like an existing brand |
| R | Restrictive | Limits future growth |
| A | Annoying | Forced, excessive wordplay |
| T | Tame | Flat, descriptive, uninspired |
| C | Curse of Knowledge | Speaks only to insiders |
| H | Hard to pronounce | People avoid saying it → avoid you |

### Sound Symbolism Quick Guide
| Sound | Psychological impact | Example names |
|---|---|---|
| V | Vibrant, alive | Corvette, Vercel, Viagra |
| B | Reliable, solid | BlackBerry |
| Z | Attention-getting | Azure, Zara |
| X | Innovative | Xerox, SpaceX |
| Plosives (b, c, k, p) | Memorable | Coca-Cola, Kodak |
| Soft sounds (l, m, n) | Approachable | Lululemon, Amazon |

### The Naming Matrix
Position candidates on two axes: **Approach** (descriptive → abstract) and **Construct** (real-word → coined).
```
                    CONSTRUCT
         Real-Word    Compound      Coined
Abstract    Roku       YouTube       Xerox
            Apple      Snapchat      Kodak
Suggestive  Amazon     Airbnb        Spotify
            Slack      Pinterest     Verizon
Descriptive PayPal     Salesforce    (rare)
            General    TripAdvisor
            Motors
```
Sweet spot for most startups: **Suggestive** (Netflix, Airbnb, Slack) — enough clarity to be graspable, enough room to grow beyond a literal description.

### Marty Neumeier's 8 Criteria
Distinctive · Brief · Appropriate · Easy to spell · Easy to pronounce · Likeable · Extendible · Protectable.

### Catchword's 10 Touchstones
Available (.com/social handles) · Trademarkable · Memorable · Scalable · Short · Positive affect · Good for SEO · Easy to pronounce · Easy to spell · Distinctive.

### Generation techniques
- **Three Words** — each stakeholder writes 3 words describing the brand; mine the overlaps and near-misses.
- **Syllable Recombination** — cut relevant words into syllables, recombine at random, keep what survives a read-aloud test.
- **Root Word Mining** — Greek/Latin roots for a credible, timeless feel (Xerox from "xeros" — dry).
- **Portmanteau** — blend two words (Pinterest = Pin + Interest).
- **Sound Symbolism** — choose letters/sounds that evoke the target feeling (see table above).
- **Free Association** — idioms, proverbs, song lyrics, foreign-language words.
- **Novel Spelling** — phonetically equivalent alternate spellings (careful: check against the "Spelling-challenged" SCRATCH flag before falling in love with one).

**Key principle:** generate 1,000+ raw candidates for a serious professional naming project before evaluating — quantity precedes quality. For a fast/informal brief, still generate at minimum 15-25 before filtering; three names is a brainstorm, not a naming process.

### The Naming Funnel
```
1,000-3,000 candidates   ← wide generation
   200-300 initial screen ← basic criteria (SMILE/SCRATCH)
       30 for availability ← domain/trademark checks
     10-15 for presentation ← full vetting
        5-7 vetted options ← final decision
```
Scale this down proportionally for a lighter-weight brief, but keep the *shape* — wide generation, then progressively harder filters, never generation-equals-presentation.

### Availability & trademark check — actually run this, don't just describe it

This is a real step with a real tool call, not background knowledge to mention. Once the SMILE/SCRATCH filter has narrowed to a shortlist (roughly 5-10 names), **web_search each finalist** before presenting the shortlist as final:

1. **Domain signal** — search `"[name].com"` and the product category together; a search that surfaces an unrelated existing business or a parked/for-sale domain page is a signal worth flagging, even though live availability needs a registrar check Claude can't perform directly. Priority order once checking: `[name].com` → `[name].io` (strong for tech) → `[name].co` → `get[name].com` / `[name]app.com` as fallbacks.
2. **Trademark/existing-brand conflict** — search `"[name]" trademark` and `"[name]" [industry/category]` for each finalist. A same-category existing company or registered mark is a hard SCRATCH "Copycat" failure, not a minor note — drop it from the shortlist rather than flagging it as a caveat.
3. **Social handle sanity check** — a quick search for the name plus "instagram" or "twitter"/"x" to see if it's already a visibly active handle in an unrelated space.

**Report what the searches actually showed**, finalist by finalist — "clear in searches, no conflicting trademark or same-category business found" is a legitimate and useful result, not a non-answer. Flag anything ambiguous rather than asserting a name is available when the search didn't actually confirm that (Claude cannot check live registrar/trademark-office databases directly — frame findings as "no conflict surfaced in search" rather than a guaranteed-clear legal opinion, and note that a formal trademark/registrar check is still worth doing before real money goes in, e.g. an MCA name search and IP India trademark search for an Indian private limited company, or the equivalent registrar in the relevant country).

### Common mistakes (all verticals)
Generic/descriptive names that fail to differentiate · falling in love before checking availability · cultural blind spots (a name with a negative meaning in another relevant language) · overly complex spelling/pronunciation · trendy suffixes ("-ify," "-io," "-ly") that date quickly · testing purely for likeability, which tends to produce bland winners over distinctive ones.

### Quick evaluation pass (use for critiquing an existing name too)
1. Read it out loud — does it flow, or catch?
2. Run SMILE — does it hit at least 3 of the 5?
3. Run SCRATCH — any red flags? Even one is often a reason to keep it on the "maybe" pile rather than the finalist pile.
4. Check the Naming Matrix position — is it where this vertical wants to sit (see "By vertical" above)?
5. Run the availability & trademark check above before getting attached — or before telling someone else their existing name is safe.
