# Typography by Archetype

Always give a **display/heading font + body font pairing**, never a single font. Real, specific typeface names — with a free/Google Fonts substitute noted when the ideal pick is a paid license, since most users need something they can actually use today.

The single biggest tell of AI-slop branding is defaulting to **Inter for everything** (or Inter + Poppins, or Montserrat + Open Sans) regardless of what the brand is. Those are fine, overused, low-personality fonts — treat them as a last resort, not a default.

---

### 1. Corporate Systematic
- Heading: **IBM Plex Sans** (or Inter, but Plex has more character while still reading corporate) · Body: **IBM Plex Sans** / **Source Sans 3**
- Logic: a typeface family designed explicitly for systematic, multi-product use — built for this exact job.

### 2. AI-Native Minimal
- Heading: **Söhne** (paid — free alt: **General Sans** or **Inter Tight**) · Body: **Tiempos Text** (paid serif — free alt: **Lora** or **Source Serif 4**) for a warm serif/sans mix, or stay sans-only with **Styrene**-alternative **Instrument Sans**
- Logic: the serif-in-body or humanist-warmth move is what makes AI-native brands feel considered instead of clinical — pure geometric sans-on-sans reads more corporate/startup-generic.

### 3. Quirky-Technical / Indie Dev
- Heading: **Departure Mono** or **Berkeley Mono** (paid — free alt: **JetBrains Mono** or **Space Mono**) for a monospace-driven wordmark · Body: **Inter** or **Geist** (fine here — this archetype's personality lives in the mono accents and copy, not in fighting the body font)
- Logic: monospace signals "made by people who write code," even used sparingly (in the logo, headers, or code-adjacent UI) rather than everywhere.

### 4. Streetwear / Mascot Rebel
- Heading/Logo: a **slab serif** (**Roboto Slab**, **Zilla Slab**) or a **graffiti/marker display face** (**Permanent Marker** used sparingly, or a licensed hand-lettered face for the actual logotype) · Body: **Archivo** or **Work Sans** for legibility once the loud logo has done its job
- Logic: the logo/heading gets to be loud and hand-crafted; body copy needs to stay boringly legible or the whole thing becomes unreadable merch copy.

### 5. Stylish Performance-Tech
- Heading: **Eurostile**-alternative **Michroma** or **Rajdhani** (condensed, angular, technical feel) · Body: **Titillium Web** or **Chakra Petch**
- Logic: condensed/angular type mimics the geometry of the visual mark (chevrons, diagonals) — heading and mark should feel cut from the same shape language.

### 6. Playful Edtech / Student
- Heading: **Feather Bold**-alternative **Baloo 2** or **Fredoka** (rounded, friendly, high personality) · Body: **Nunito Sans** or **Mulish**
- Logic: rounded terminals throughout (heading and body both) reinforce the "friendly/safe" feeling — a sharp/geometric body font next to a rounded heading undercuts the warmth.

### 7. Warm Food & Hospitality
- Heading: a **script or humanist serif accent** (**Caveat** or **Kalam** for hand-touched menus, **Fraunces** for a more editorial food-brand feel) · Body: **Karla** or **Work Sans** for actual menu/UI legibility
- Logic: reserve the script/display face for short moments (logotype, section headers) — an entire menu set in script is unreadable; that's a rookie food-brand mistake.

### 8. Heritage / Craft
- Heading: **Fraunces** (has warmth and a slightly vintage soul) or, for Indian-rooted brands, a **modernized Devanagari-Latin hybrid** typeface where available · Body: **Source Serif 4** or **Lora**
- Logic: serif throughout signals "printed matter, tradition, history" — sans-serif-only tends to read modern/startup regardless of color choices, which fights this archetype's whole point.

### 9. Civic / Hackathon Energy
- Heading: **Anton** or **Archivo Black** (bold, condensed, reads from a distance) · Body: **Archivo** or **Work Sans**
- Logic: needs to survive being blown up on a stage banner and shrunk to a badge — pick the boldest, most geometrically simple face that still has personality.

### 10. Fintech Trust
- Heading: **General Sans** or **Aeonik**-alternative **Sora** · Body: **Inter** (acceptable here — fintech UI genuinely benefits from Inter's neutrality and numeral legibility)
- Logic: this is one of the few archetypes where a "neutral" font is actually correct — you want the *product* to feel exciting, not the typography; numerals/tabular figures matter more than personality here.

### 11. Brutalist / Indie Maximalist
- Heading: **Helvetica Now** / **Neue Haas Grotesk** at an extreme weight (Black) and extreme size, or a raw system font used unapologetically (even **Arial** can work here, on purpose) · Body: same family, regular weight
- Logic: one typeface family used at extreme, confrontational scale is more brutalist than mixing multiple "interesting" fonts — restraint in font *choice*, maximalism in font *size/weight*.

### 12. Luxury Minimal
- Heading: **Canela**-alternative **Fraunces** (light weight) or a thin-weight geometric sans like **Neue Montreal** · Body: same family, light-to-regular weight only
- Logic: heavy weights read cheap/loud in this archetype — everything stays light, which is part of why it feels expensive (confidence without needing to shout).

---

## Pairing logic, in general

- **Contrast, don't clash:** pair a serif with a sans, or a display face with a neutral workhorse — two display faces together usually fight each other.
- **One font family does the heavy lifting for body text** across the whole product/site — variety in *weight*, not in *typeface*, keeps things calm.
- **Match the era:** a face designed in the last 5 years (most "AI-native" and geometric sans picks) paired with a face with real history (a classic serif) reads more interesting than two contemporary faces together.
- **Test at logo scale AND at body-text scale** before finalizing — a display face that looks great as a 200px logotype can be unreadable at 14px, and vice versa.
